## 1. KYIV / POLAND — Russia's biggest strike; missiles land in Poland (hero story)
- Russia launched its largest missile-and-drone strike of November on the
  night of 19–20 Nov: 404 Shahed/Gerbera drones and 111 missiles,
  including 27 ballistic missiles, some with cluster warheads. At least
  eight people were killed in Kyiv, more than two dozen wounded; bridges
  across the Dnipro and railway stations were damaged.
- Two Russian missiles and a suspected drone came down in Poland. A
  Kh-101 cruise missile crashed near Nowy Lipowiec / Rudnik nad Sanem,
  about 60 km from the Ukrainian border and ~35 km from the Coalition of
  the Willing exercise at Nowa Deba. No injuries or damage in Poland.
- Polish PM Donald Tusk called it "an intentional provocation" and said
  it was no coincidence it came on the exercise's VIP day; NATO chief
  Mark Rutte called it a "grave violation" of Polish airspace. Poland
  asked for more NATO forces and air defence. President Zelenskyy called
  the attack one of the largest since the war began.

## 2. NEW YORK — AI sell-off: S&P and Dow fall into correction
- US stocks plunged on Friday 20 Nov as the AI trade unravelled. The
  Dow fell 950 points (-1.93%) to 48,147 and the S&P 500 fell 3.7% to
  6,910 — both now more than 10% below their record highs, in correction
  territory. The Nasdaq sank 5.28% to 22,617, its worst week since March
  2020, wiping out all its 2026 gains; the S&P had its seventh straight
  down day, its longest losing streak since 2020.
- Nvidia fell 14% on the day (-23% for the week) amid an SEC accounting
  probe and the resignation of CEO Jensen Huang. Anthropic, which debuted
  Thursday at $83 and closed up 17% at $97.17, fell 9.1% on Friday to
  $88.30, close to its IPO price.

## 3. BRUSSELS — UK and EU sign biggest post-Brexit reset
- PM Andy Burnham and EU leaders signed three agreements at the Brussels
  summit on Friday 20 Nov: an end to most post-Brexit checks on food and
  farm exports, a link between the UK and EU carbon markets, and a youth
  experience scheme — 100,000 visas a year for 18- to 31-year-olds to
  live, work or study for up to three years.
- Burnham said the package could add £17bn a year to the UK economy by
  2040 and "draw a line on a decade of drift and division". Von der Leyen
  called it a "turning point". But EU states said the deal will only take
  effect once the UK agrees to cut university tuition fees for EU
  students — six months of talks ahead.

## 4. ANTALYA — COP31 runs into overtime as fossil fuels vanish from draft
- The UN COP31 climate talks in Antalya, Turkey, ran past their Friday
  deadline into overtime, with nearly 200 nations split over a draft
  final deal that makes no mention of fossil fuels at all — the Thursday
  draft, released by lead negotiator Australia, dropped Monday's
  references to phasing out oil, gas and coal after objections from
  Saudi Arabia and other producers. A third draft was promised for
  Saturday morning.
- Activists and leaders called it potentially the "worst COP ever";
  Panama's environment minister said the talks were held hostage by a
  "petroleum mafia". The draft does acknowledge warming will overshoot
  the 1.5C Paris goal, and drops a $120bn adaptation-finance target.

## 5. RIYADH — Saudi defences down three Houthi missiles, third night of alerts
- The Saudi-led coalition said early on Saturday 21 Nov it intercepted
  and destroyed three ballistic missiles fired by Yemen's Houthis towards
  Riyadh, after a civil defence alert at 1:53 a.m. and two explosions
  heard in the capital. No damage or casualties were reported.
- It was the third consecutive night of emergency alerts in Riyadh, two
  days after a Houthi missile damaged the 80-storey PIF tower on
  Thursday. The Houthis are now threatening ports and a power station.

## 7. LOS ANGELES — GTA VI makes $3.79bn in a day, the biggest launch ever

  Auto VI took $3.79 billion in retail sales in its first 24 hours —

- The day-one haul is nearly five times the estimated $800m that GTA V
